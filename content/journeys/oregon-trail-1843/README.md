# oregon-trail-1843

The Oregon emigration of 1843 (the "Great Migration"): the rendezvous west of
Independence (18 May) to Oregon City (13 November; the journey runs on to the 30th
while the families come in). Rebuilt to the Napoleon standard in November 2026
(ROADMAP R3).

The title avoids "The Oregon Trail" on its own, which is a game trademark
(HANDOFF.md §11).

**Source of truth:** [`authoring.json`](./authoring.json). `journey.json`, `bundle.json`
and `routes/*.geojson` are generated by `pnpm content:build`.

## What is here (version 2)

- **The wagons** track: one wagon party's camps, night by night (116 stops). To Fort
  Walla Walla it is James Nesmith's wagon, from his diary; beyond, the main body's
  wagons, then rafts and boats. `dailyRhythm` 07:00–18:00.
- **`trail.geojson`**: the National Park Service's surveyed line of the Oregon
  National Historic Trail (public domain), minus the Barlow Road of 1846.
  `pnpm --filter @living-journeys/content trace oregon-trail-1843` cuts each leg from
  it into `roads.json`. Camps within 10 km of the line were moved onto it.
- One stat: deaths named in the sources (a step at each).
- 98 moments, 52 with a line from a diary or letter; 26 timed episodes; 29 conditions.

## Editing rules

Same as Napoleon (see its README). West longitudes are negative, so local times are
*later* in UTC (at Fort Laramie, 07:00 is 13:58Z). Re-run the trace after moving a
vertex. Vertices west of Fort Walla Walla are marked `"straight": true`: the wagons
left the surveyed trail there.

## research/

Two research files (to Fort Hall; from Fort Hall). The moment texts of the second
were put into the past tense to match the first; nothing else was changed.

Known soft spots:

- Most nightly camps are dead-reckoned from Nesmith's miles, then snapped to the
  trail. Landmark coordinates were not checked against an NPS or OCTA table.
- The South Platte crossing ("Sleepy Grove") is a reconstruction from three mileage
  statements, perhaps 10–15 miles out. Ash Hollow is left out on purpose.
- Fort Walla Walla to Oregon City has no wagon diary. Dates are Frémont's, who
  followed two weeks behind; the days at the Cascades and Fort Vancouver are estimates.
- The drowning is shown on 2 November; the sources fix it only to 29 October –
  4 November.
- Some days are long for ox teams (32 miles into Fort Bridger on 14 August): the
  camps either side are interpolated from Burnett, not logged.
- Newby, Boardman, Reading, Talbot and Whitman's letters were not read directly;
  Newby is quoted from excerpts.
- No temperature stat: the only readings are Frémont's, weeks behind the wagons.

`poc-seed.json` is the original proof-of-concept data, kept for provenance.
